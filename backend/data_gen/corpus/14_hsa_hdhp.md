# Saver HDHP Plans & Health Savings Accounts (HSA)

Saver HDHP plans are **high-deductible health plans** that qualify you to open and contribute to a **Health Savings Account (HSA)** — a tax-advantaged account you own and keep even if you change plans or jobs.

## How Saver HDHP cost sharing works
- **Preventive care** (including prenatal care) is covered at $0 before the deductible.
- **All other services and prescriptions** are paid at the full negotiated (allowed) price until you meet the deductible. There are no copays.
- After the deductible, you pay **coinsurance** (for example 20–35%) until you reach the out-of-pocket maximum; then the plan pays 100% for the rest of the year.
- Insulin and select chronic-condition drugs (e.g., inhaled asthma medications, statins for heart-disease prevention) may be covered before the deductible under IRS Notice 2019-45.
- Telehealth services are subject to the deductible.

## 2026 IRS limits
| Item | Self-only coverage | Family coverage |
|---|---|---|
| HSA contribution limit | **$4,400** | **$8,750** |
| Catch-up contribution (age 55+) | +$1,000 | +$1,000 |
| Minimum HDHP deductible | $1,700 | $3,400 |
| Maximum HDHP out-of-pocket | $8,500 | $17,000 |

Contributions for a tax year can be made until the federal tax filing deadline (April 15 of the following year).

## HSA tax advantages ("triple tax advantage")
1. Contributions are **tax-deductible** (or pre-tax through payroll).
2. Investment growth and interest are **tax-free**.
3. Withdrawals for **qualified medical expenses** are **tax-free**.

## Qualified medical expenses
Deductibles, coinsurance, prescriptions, dental and vision care, hearing aids, and many over-the-counter medicines and menstrual products qualify. Non-qualified withdrawals before age 65 are taxed as income plus a **20% penalty**; after 65 they are taxed as income only.

## Eligibility rules
To contribute to an HSA you must be covered by an HSA-qualified HDHP, have no other disqualifying coverage (such as a general-purpose FSA or Medicare), and not be claimed as a dependent on someone else's tax return.
