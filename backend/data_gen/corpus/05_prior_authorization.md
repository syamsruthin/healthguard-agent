# Prior Authorization & Utilization Management

Prior authorization (PA, also called pre-certification) is approval from HealthGuard **before** you receive certain services or drugs. It confirms the service is medically necessary and covered under your plan.

Emergency services **never** require prior authorization. For an emergency inpatient admission, the hospital must notify HealthGuard within 24 hours (or the next business day).

## Who requests it

Your **in-network provider** submits the request through the HealthGuard provider portal (or by fax with clinical notes). If you use an out-of-network provider on a Choice PPO or Saver HDHP plan, you are responsible for making sure authorization is obtained.

## Decision timelines (2026)

| Request type | Decision within |
|---|---|
| Standard pre-service request | **7 calendar days** |
| Expedited (urgent) request | **72 hours** |
| Concurrent review (ongoing inpatient stay) | 24 hours |
| Retrospective review (after service) | 30 calendar days |

Approvals list the approved service, number of units/visits and a **valid-through date** (typically 60–90 days for procedures and 12 months for drugs). If the service is not performed before that date, a new request is needed.

## What happens if authorization is missing

The claim is denied with code **D002 — prior authorization not obtained**. If the provider is in-network, **you cannot be billed** for the denied amount. Your provider may request a retrospective review within 30 days of the denial, or you may file a Level 1 appeal.

## Status meanings

- **pending** — received and under clinical review.
- **approved** — authorized as requested.
- **partially_approved** — fewer units/days than requested were authorized.
- **denied** — not authorized; the letter explains the clinical criteria and your appeal rights.
- **withdrawn** — cancelled by the requesting provider.

## Step therapy

For selected drugs, HealthGuard requires that you first try one or more lower-cost, clinically appropriate drugs (the "steps"). If the pharmacy claim is submitted before the steps are met it is rejected with **D011 — step therapy requirement not met**. Your prescriber can submit a **step therapy exception** when:
- you already tried the required drug(s) (including under a previous insurer) and they failed or were not tolerated;
- the required drug is contraindicated; or
- you are stable on the requested drug.
Exception requests are decided within 72 hours (24 hours when urgent).

## Peer-to-peer review

Before or after a denial, the ordering provider may speak with a HealthGuard medical director about the case. Many denials for missing clinical information are overturned this way.
